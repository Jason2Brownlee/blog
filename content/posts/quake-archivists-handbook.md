I've been writing and finally released a new book:

**[The Quake Archivist's Handbook](https://jasonbrownlee.me/QuakeArchivistsHandbook/)**: _How to Find Lost Mods, Recover Forgotten Files, and Preserve Quake History_.

I'm very excited about it.

Writing it helped to make my processes more systematic and in turn I discovered a bunch of rare files for my archives. Win!

Here's the blurb from the back cover:

> **Somewhere out there, a 30-year-old Quake ZIP file is waiting to be discovered.**
>
> Maybe it's buried on a forgotten FTP mirror. Hidden inside an old magazine CD. Or sitting on a retired developer's hard drive.
>
> Finding it is a treasure hunt. And this book shows you how.
>
> *The Quake Archivist's Handbook* is a practical guide to discovering, recovering, documenting and preserving the thousands of mods, bots, maps and utilities created for Quake, Quake II and Quake III Arena.
>
> Drawing on more than five years of hands-on archival experience, Jason Brownlee shares the methods, tools and resources he uses to recover lost files and reconstruct the histories of forgotten modifications.
>
> Learn how to explore the forgotten internet, search historical file archives, track down original developers, automate your research and build permanent public archives.
>
> Whether you're a nostalgic Quake player, a curious researcher or an aspiring digital archivist, this handbook gives you the knowledge to start preserving a small piece of gaming history.
>
> **Pick a modification. Follow the clues. Start the hunt.**
